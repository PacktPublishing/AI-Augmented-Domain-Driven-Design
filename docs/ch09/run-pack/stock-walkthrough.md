# BrewUp Warehouse — Field Interview

Raw evidence for domain discovery.

**This is a synthetic fixture. It is not a record of an interview that took
place.** Nadia and Andrea are fictional composites, and the brewery floor
described here is a construction. Treat the text as the evidence you have been
given, and cite it as written; do not treat it as testimony from real people.

The text is presented as an unedited transcript. Where the two speakers
contradict each other, the contradiction stands. Where someone trails off or
says they do not know, that stands too.

Every line carries a stable identifier. `QUOTE-nn` marks something a participant
said. `OBS-nn` marks a note written by the interviewer while observing. Cite
these identifiers when referring to the evidence.

---

**OBS-01** — The warehouse is one long room behind the brewhouse. Pallets of
bottled beer down the left wall, kegs on racks at the far end, a chilled room at
the back for anything that has to stay cold. A desk with a screen sits near the
door. Nadia works the floor; Andrea works the screen.

**OBS-02** — There is a whiteboard next to the desk, ruled into columns by hand.
Each row has a scribbled number, a product code, a quantity, and sometimes a
date. Several rows have been rubbed out and written over. Nobody offered an
explanation for the board until asked.

---

**QUOTE-01** — *Interviewer:* Start at the beginning. How does work reach you?

**QUOTE-02** — *Andrea:* It comes through from Sales. Something lands on my screen
that says a customer has put an order in, here is the number, here is what they
want, how many of each. I do not talk to the customer. I have never talked to a
customer.

**QUOTE-03** — *Nadia:* And it is not up to us whether they get it. We are told
somebody wants twelve cases of the pale ale. Our job is to say whether twelve
cases of the pale ale can be had.

**QUOTE-04** — *Interviewer:* And when they can?

**QUOTE-05** — *Nadia:* Then I set them aside. I physically walk down, count them
off, and move them to the end bay so nobody else takes them. Then Andrea writes it
on the board.

**QUOTE-06** — *Andrea:* I put a hold on it. Order number, product code, how many,
and I chalk the date next to it.

**QUOTE-07** — *Interviewer:* You said "set aside" and you said "put a hold on".
Is that the same thing?

**QUOTE-08** — *Nadia:* Same thing. I earmark them, he holds them. It is the same
beer either way. He is doing the paperwork half of what I am doing with my hands.

**OBS-03** — Three different words were used for one action inside four lines:
*set aside*, *put a hold on*, *earmark*. Neither speaker appeared to notice, and
both treated the words as interchangeable. Later in the interview Andrea used
"set aside" and Nadia used "hold", so the usage is not split by role either.

**QUOTE-09** — *Interviewer:* How do you know whether you have enough?

**QUOTE-10** — *Andrea:* The screen has a number against every product code. But
the number on the screen is not the number I can promise, because some of it is
already spoken for on the board.

**QUOTE-11** — *Nadia:* That is the part people get wrong. There is what is
standing in the room and there is what is still going. They are not the same
number and they have never been the same number.

**QUOTE-12** — *Andrea:* So I take what the screen says and I take off whatever is
sitting on the board against that code. What is left is what I can hold for the
next one.

---

**QUOTE-13** — *Interviewer:* What happens when there is not enough?

**QUOTE-14** — *Andrea:* Then I cannot hold it. I put it back as not done and it
goes back to Sales.

**QUOTE-15** — *Interviewer:* All of it? Suppose they want twelve and you have
eight.

**QUOTE-16** — *Andrea:* Then it is a no. Twelve was the ask. I have not got
twelve.

**QUOTE-17** — *Nadia:* That is not what I do. If they want twelve and I have
eight I will put the eight in the end bay, because eight is going out either way
and I would rather have them counted. Then somebody rings Sales.

**QUOTE-18** — *Interviewer:* So which of those is the rule?

**QUOTE-19** — *Nadia:* [laughs] Whichever of us is in that day.

**QUOTE-20** — *Andrea:* It has never been written down. I do it my way because
that is how I was shown. She does it her way because she has been here longer
than the screen has.

**OBS-04** — Pressed twice more on this point. Neither speaker would name a rule,
and neither treated the other's practice as wrong. There does not appear to be a
decision on record anywhere about the short-quantity case.

---

**QUOTE-21** — *Interviewer:* You said a no goes back to Sales. Is every no the
same kind of no?

**QUOTE-22** — *Andrea:* No, and it annoys me that it looks the same on the
screen. Sometimes it is a no because we are genuinely out, we have sold it, it
is gone, there is more coming Thursday. Sometimes it is a no because the code on
the order is not a beer we make. Somebody has typed it wrong, or it is an old
code from a recipe we dropped.

**QUOTE-23** — *Nadia:* Those are not the same problem at all. One of them you
wait for. The other one somebody has to go and fix.

**QUOTE-24** — *Interviewer:* Does the message you send back say which it is?

**QUOTE-25** — *Andrea:* It says it could not be done. That is all it says.

---

**QUOTE-26** — *Interviewer:* When it is the first kind — you are out, more is
coming Thursday — who tries again?

**QUOTE-27** — *Nadia:* Somebody does.

**QUOTE-28** — *Interviewer:* Who?

**QUOTE-29** — *Nadia:* Honestly? Depends. If I know the delivery is coming and
I remember whose order it was, I will go and do it myself on the Thursday.
Sometimes Sales rings down and asks us to look again. Sometimes I think nobody
does and the customer rings up cross.

**QUOTE-30** — *Andrea:* I would not do it off my own back. It is not my order.

**QUOTE-31** — *Interviewer:* Is there anything that says whose job it is?

**QUOTE-32** — *Andrea:* Not that anyone has shown me.

---

**QUOTE-33** — *Interviewer:* Tell me about the dates on the board.

**QUOTE-34** — *Nadia:* We do not hold a thing forever. If it sits in the end bay
and nothing happens, eventually it comes off and goes back into the room.

**QUOTE-35** — *Interviewer:* After how long?

**QUOTE-36** — *Nadia:* A while. I clear the board when it starts looking silly.

**QUOTE-37** — *Andrea:* She clears it Fridays mostly.

**QUOTE-38** — *Nadia:* Mostly. If it is a big customer I will leave it up longer
and if it is somebody who messes us about I will have it off there quicker.

**QUOTE-39** — *Interviewer:* So there is no set period.

**QUOTE-40** — *Nadia:* There is not, and I have said for years there ought to
be, because when I am on holiday nobody clears it at all and then we are holding
beer against rows nobody has called about for a month. What became of those
orders I could not tell you. Nobody comes down and tells us.

**QUOTE-41** — *Andrea:* Nobody has ever written down how long. I asked when I
started. I was told "you will get a feel for it".

---

**QUOTE-42** — *Interviewer:* What about orders that get called off?

**QUOTE-43** — *Andrea:* That comes through from Sales as well. Order such-and-such
is off. Then I rub it off the board and Nadia puts the beer back in the room.

**QUOTE-44** — *Nadia:* And the moment it is back in the room it is anybody's
again. That is the whole point of putting it back.

**QUOTE-45** — *Interviewer:* Is that different from you clearing the board on a
Friday?

**QUOTE-46** — *Andrea:* The beer ends up in the same place. But one of them is
somebody telling us, and the other one is us deciding because time has passed.

---

**QUOTE-47** — *Interviewer:* Do you know whether the customer has paid?

**QUOTE-48** — *Andrea:* No idea. Never seen it on my screen and I would not know
where to look.

**QUOTE-49** — *Nadia:* That is the office. We have put beer aside for orders
that turned out never to be paid for, and we only found out because it sat
there.

---

**QUOTE-50** — *Interviewer:* What happens after you have set beer aside?

**QUOTE-51** — *Nadia:* At some point the order goes through properly and the
shipping lads come and take it off the end bay onto a van. That is them, not us.
I do not book a van and I would not know how.

**QUOTE-52** — *Interviewer:* Who decides the order goes through?

**QUOTE-53** — *Nadia:* Sales. Always Sales. We tell them the beer is there. What
they do with that is theirs.

**QUOTE-54** — *Andrea:* We are one of the things they wait on. I do not know what
the others are.

---

**QUOTE-55** — *Interviewer:* Anything that goes wrong that we have not covered?

**QUOTE-56** — *Nadia:* The one that upsets everybody is when a batch fails its
check after we have already put it aside. It happened in the spring. The lab
came back on a batch of stout and said it was not going out, and we had four
different orders holding beer out of that batch.

**QUOTE-57** — *Interviewer:* What did you do?

**QUOTE-58** — *Nadia:* We took it off the board and told the office and they
sorted it. I could not tell you what the right thing to do is. I do not think we
have ever agreed one. It has only happened the once since I have been here.

**OBS-05** — The batch-failure case was raised unprompted at the end and appears
to be genuinely unsettled. It is the only topic on which both speakers deferred
to "the office" without describing any warehouse practice at all.

**OBS-06** — Throughout the interview, everything concerning payment, order
approval, and delivery reached the warehouse second-hand. Neither speaker
described performing any of those actions, and both described being told about
them or handing them on.
