# BrewUp — Working Vocabulary

Words used inside the brewery. This list exists so that the interview transcript
can be read by someone who has not stood in the building. It is not a model, and
it is not a list of the concepts a model should contain.

Each word is listed as it was heard. Where two entries appear to describe
related activity, this list does not say whether they are one thing.

---

**Batch** — One production run of a beer. Everything bottled or kegged from that
run shares a batch identifier and is tested together.

**Case** — A carton of bottles, the unit most customer orders are counted in.

**Keg** — A pressurised container of beer, sold to pubs and bars.

**Pallet** — A stack of cases as it stands in the warehouse.

**End bay** — The rack nearest the loading door, used for beer that has been
separated from general stock for a particular customer order.

**The board** — A whiteboard beside the warehouse desk. Rows are written and
rubbed out by hand. Each row is written against a customer order.

**The screen** — The warehouse terminal. Shows what has arrived from Sales and a
quantity figure against each product code.

**Product code** — The short code identifying a beer, for example `PA-500`.
Codes for discontinued recipes remain in circulation on old paperwork.

**The office** — Used by warehouse staff for the administrative side of the
business, including anything to do with money.

**Sales** — The part of BrewUp that deals with customers and takes their orders.

**The shipping lads** — The staff who load vans and take goods out to customers.

**The lab** — The staff who test a batch and decide whether it may be sold.

**To set aside** — Used in the interview for separating beer from general stock
against a particular customer order.

**To put a hold on** — Used in the interview for writing an order number, product
code and quantity onto the board.

**To earmark** — Used in the interview for beer separated against a particular
customer order.

**To put back** — Returning beer from the end bay into general stock.

**To clear the board** — Rubbing out rows on the whiteboard.

**Short** — Not having as much of something as has been asked for.
